Design Partner Brief / v0.1

PRE-OPERATIONAL COLLABORATION

Help define the system before deployment defines the rules.

VULK is recruiting a small number of design partners to shape governable, edge-first public-safety intelligence around real operator needs and explicit civil-liberties boundaries.

Start a conversation Review the model ↓

The boundary

A design partnership is not permission for open-ended surveillance. It begins with one approved use case, a named operator workflow, and written limits.

A bounded engagement

One use case.
One policy profile.
One workflow.

The objective is not maximum feature coverage. It is to test whether useful intelligence and enforceable governance can coexist in a real operating context.

  1. 01

    DISCOVER

    Define the mission and limits

    Map the decision, approved sensor inputs, prohibited uses, retention boundary, authorization chain, and operator pain points.

  2. 02

    MODEL

    Translate policy into controls

    Produce a deployment policy profile, audit requirements, human-authorization gates, and integration plan before connecting operational systems.

  3. 03

    EXERCISE

    Run a controlled scenario

    Evaluate the workflow using approved synthetic, historical, or controlled data. Live sensitive data is not required for the initial engagement.

  4. 04

    DECIDE

    Document evidence and gaps

    Review operational usefulness, false-positive burden, policy compliance, auditability, integration friction, and go/no-go criteria for a future pilot.

What the work produces

Artifacts, not promises.

01 / MISSION

Use-case and threat model

A shared description of the safety problem, operating context, misuse risks, and success criteria.

02 / GOVERNANCE

Deployment policy profile

Defined data sources, access roles, retention rules, authorization gates, prohibited uses, and audit events.

03 / OPERATIONS

Operator workflow

A reviewable flow from detection to evidence review, escalation, human decision, and documented outcome.

04 / EVIDENCE

Evaluation record

Observed strengths, failure modes, operator feedback, unresolved risks, and requirements for any future pilot.

Who should participate

Bring operations and oversight to the same table.

Strong fit

  • A public-safety agency or infrastructure operator with a defined operational problem
  • An accountable operational owner who can describe the current workflow
  • A governance, privacy, legal, or community stakeholder willing to define limits
  • A team prepared to challenge assumptions and document failure modes

Not a fit

  • Open-ended collection without a defined public-safety purpose
  • Hidden deployments or systems intended to avoid meaningful oversight
  • Autonomous consequential decisions without an accountable human
  • Requests to claim operational maturity, certifications, or results that do not exist
